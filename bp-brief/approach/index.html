Approach · 02 of 04

Design for our own shrinking footprint, from day one.

One stance threaded through six answers — how we'd respond to BP's RFI as one argument, not six independent positions.

The argument

Most engagements grow. This one would be designed to shrink.

BP has stated it explicitly: increase autonomy over time, reduce dependency on partners. Most agency engagements grow in the opposite direction — staffing expands, scope creeps, the partner becomes load-bearing. The brief is asking for the inverse: a partner whose decreasing presence is engineered into the commercial model, team shape, and ways of working from week one.

That stance shapes every answer that follows. The principles, the team design, the commercial logic — none of it treats the anti-dependency requirement as a slide at the back. It is the argument the response is built around.

How we'd shape the response

Three areas of strategy, developed across the six answers.

COMMERCIAL STRATEGY
Direction and challenge.
How we'd challenge BP constructively and shape direction — not just execute against a backlog. Where the response shows thought leadership rather than capability listing.
Question 3 · Priority opportunities

What priority opportunities should BP pursue over the next 12–24 months?

Our approach

Surface three to four hypotheses informed by public signals on bp.com and our experience with comparable estates. Each linked to a value pathway and indicative effort. Non-obvious insight prioritised over generic best-practice.

Question 4 · Near-term future

Which one or two technology or behavioural shifts in the next 24–36 months should BP prepare for now?

Our approach

Connect long-term shifts to specific near-term decisions BP can act on this year — avoiding generic futurism, grounding each shift in a 24-month decision that already needs to be made.

EXPERIENCE STRATEGY
Craft and platform.
How we'd deliver enterprise-scale digital experiences on Contentful — and keep complexity from running away from us as the wider estate migrates over.
Question 1 · Customer experience

What principles and ways of working enable high-quality digital experiences on modern platforms, at organisations of BP's scale and complexity?

Our approach

Lead with two case spotlights showing measurable customer outcomes and the operating disciplines behind them — design systems, decision rituals, and ways of working that survived contact with enterprise scale. Honest reflection on what we'd do differently sits inside each example.

Question 2 · Contentful proficiency

How do we model content, govern change, and support multi-team contribution at enterprise scale on Contentful?

Our approach

Lay out the opinionated patterns we hold across Contentful engagements — content model discipline, environment hygiene, role-based contribution flows. Before-and-after simplification examples show how we reduce complexity over time, not accumulate it.

ORGANISATIONAL STRATEGY
Partnership and autonomy.
How we'd create productivity and capability without creating new dependencies — the area where the response is most directly tested against the brief's anti-dependency framing.
Question 5 · AI & efficiency

How do we deliver differentiated AI value and measurable productivity without creating proprietary or opaque dependencies?

Our approach

Show concrete reusable patterns from content ops, QA, and localisation engagements — open, inspectable, and replicable by BP teams. Measured productivity gains and AI governance shared as transferable IP, not retained capability.

Question 6 · Operating model

How do we build BP's internal capability, governance, and operating models — and reduce dependency on partners over time?

Our approach

Describe a staged shrinkage curve: co-delivery → embedded coaching → background presence. Role clarity, anti-dependency tactics, and explicit capability transfer milestones — including which capabilities sit in-house versus with partners across the lifecycle.

What shapes every answer

Five principles that show up in every response.

01
Opinionated patterns, not bespoke complexity.
Contentful done well is a small set of well-defended patterns, applied consistently. Every bespoke deviation is future maintenance cost and a brake on the wider estate migration. We hold the line on simplicity, even when individual stakeholders push for exceptions.
02
Designed to shrink from week one.
The team shape is engineered for the anti-dependency curve, not bolted on later. Co-delivery in year one, embedded coaching in year two, background presence by year three. Headcount, role mix, and commercial model reflect that trajectory from the start.
03
Operating model is the hard half.
The technical migration is the easy work. The harder work is moving ~30 country teams into a centralised UK–India publishing model. We name the friction early, treat it as core engagement scope, and don't let it surprise the steering committee at month six.
04
bp.com is the proof point. The wider estate is the prize.
Every pattern, content model, and governance decision is built to scale to the 200+ wider sites and apps. bp.com is where they get tested; the rest of the estate is where they earn out and where AEM finally retires.
05
AI as transparent capability, not opaque dependency.
AI accelerates content operations, QA, localisation, and experimentation — but every model, prompt, and pipeline stays inspectable and replicable by BP's own teams. Productivity without lock-in. No black boxes left behind.
What this asks of the team

A team shape engineered for the curve, not bolted onto it.

Senior strategists who can offer constructive challenge — and absorb it. Engineers who hold the line on patterns over bespoke complexity. A commercial logic that rewards transferred capability, not retained presence.

This is the work, said plainly. As Vera Schnitzlein puts it in Valtech's manufacturing leaders report: "the what of digital transformation is increasingly obvious; the how remains the true differentiator." A response that demonstrates how, not what, is the response this brief is built to reward.
