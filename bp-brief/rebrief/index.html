Rebrief · 01 of 04

BP is rebuilding its global digital estate from the ground up.

BP is replatforming its global web estate on Contentful, consolidating ~30 country sites into a Global experience plus a separate China instance, retiring Adobe AEM, and preparing the estate for AI-driven discovery. The RFI is the first of a two-stage procurement and tests how a partner thinks and partners — not only what they deliver. Six questions, 28 pages, anonymous. The chosen supplier goes live 4 January 2027.

What we need to deliver

Five outcomes a credible response has to win on.

01
Modernise the platform and design
Replace ageing on-prem Adobe AEM with a Contentful-based, API-first foundation. Refresh an 8-year-old design to align with BP's 2025 brand evolution and modern audience expectations.
02
Consolidate the estate
~30 country sites consolidate into Global plus China. Content volume drops by ~45%. A centralised UK–India publishing model replaces fragmented country teams.
03
Prepare for AI-driven discovery
Position bp.com for AI-driven search, accessibility, and omnichannel delivery. Introduce data-driven governance over content quality and lifecycle.
04
Migrate the wider estate over time
DCP is the strategic foundation for 200+ wider sites and apps across multiple countries and brands. The long-term move is to retire AEM across the whole estate.
05
Increase bp's autonomy over time
Build internal capability, governance, and operating models. Reduce dependency on partners as BP matures — knowledge transfer, role clarity, in-house versus partner placement.
Where it's won

Three things the brief doesn't say — and where the response is differentiated.

01
The consolidation is an operating-model shift, not a platform project.
~30 country sites exist because country teams want autonomy. Moving them into a centralised UK–India publishing model is the hardest part of this engagement. The technical migration is the easy half.
02
bp.com is the proof point. The wider estate is the prize.
The RFI focuses heavily on bp.com, but the strategic value sits in onboarding 200+ wider sites to DCP and retiring AEM across the estate. A partner that solves only for bp.com is solving for the smaller half.
03
"Anti-dependency" inverts the standard agency curve.
Most agency engagements grow. BP is asking for one that shrinks. That requires a different commercial logic and a different team shape from day one — not bolted on later when capability transfer becomes a slide in a steering committee.
Who wins this

What it takes to win this — and to deserve to.

A partner that brings constructive challenge as much as delivery muscle. Reads and responds to internal operating-model friction, not only platform constraints. Has Contentful depth without being dogmatic. Designs for its own diminishing footprint.

This is not an order-taker brief. The six questions and the "how you think and partner" framing make that explicit. A strong response demonstrates point of view and lessons learnt — not a list of capabilities or a portfolio of brand logos. The brief invites disagreement, calibrated and earned.
